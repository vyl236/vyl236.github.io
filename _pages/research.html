--- layout: archive title: "Research" permalink: /research/ author_profile: true --- My research examines the intersection of leadership, identity, and organizational change. I explore how news organizations, higher education institutions, and public sector organizations navigate professional identities while balancing the tensions between public service and business missions. Across these contexts, I am particularly interested in how leadership shapes organizational culture, branding, communication strategies, stakeholder relationships, and institutional change. My research philosophy is grounded in an interdisciplinary approach that integrates journalism studies, communication, sociology, and organizational studies. Drawing on these perspectives allows me to examine organizational phenomena from multiple levels, connecting individual experiences with broader institutional and societal processes. Methodologically, I am well-versed in qualitative, quantitative, and computational approaches, enabling me to select the most appropriate methods and combine them to answer complex research questions. My dissertation exemplifies this interdisciplinary and mixed-methods approach. It is one of the largest studies of leadership in journalism to date, combining 32 in-depth interviews with more than 1,000 survey responses from journalism leaders across the United States. By examining how leadership identities are developed, enacted, and recognized, the project advances our understanding of leadership in journalism while providing practical insights for leadership development and organizational practice.
